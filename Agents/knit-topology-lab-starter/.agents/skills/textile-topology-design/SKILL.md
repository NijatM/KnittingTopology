---
name: textile-topology-design
description: Create or inspect pattern-graph connectivity, textile annotations, multilayers, joins and pocket openings before machine-specific compilation.
---

State the represented object: curve network, cell mesh, pattern graph, loop/course/wale topology or scheduled program. Do not silently equate them. Assign stable graph identity and distinguish projected curve crossings from actual junctions.

For pockets, create explicit layer/patch identities, joining boundaries, orientation and opening loops. Check connectivity, components, intended boundaries and intersection policy at the relevant sheet/assembly level. Preserve these through edits and save/load. A displaced surface is not automatically a pocket.

Course/wale or loop semantics require explicit relations, not merely renaming mesh edges. Document unresolved construction constraints and permit clearly labeled exploratory topology. Machine independence is an interface goal, not evidence that every graph can be fabricated on every knitting system.
